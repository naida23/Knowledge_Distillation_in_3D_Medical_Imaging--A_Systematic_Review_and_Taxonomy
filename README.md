# Knowledge Distillation in 3D Medical Imaging — A Systematic Review and Taxonomy

This repository contains the supplementary material for our systematic review on knowledge distillation in 3D medical imaging. We systematically analyzed 112 publications (Jan 2015– May 2025) and organized them into detailed tables with information on:

- Network architecture
- Knowledge distillation loss and distillation type (response-, feature-, and relation-based)
- Receptive field, data augmentation, evaluation metrics, organs, and datasets
- Teacher–student configuration (single-/multi-teacher, online/offline/self-distillation)
- Label availability (label-required vs. label-free)
- Data setting (data-free, few-sample, and cross-modal distillation)
- Related learning paradigms (adversarial, graph-based, few-shot, incremental, reinforcement, semi-/self-supervised, EMA, and pseudo-labeling)
- Knowledge distillation purpose
- Concise one-sentence summary of each paper.

The repository is intended as a practical reference for researchers developing efficient and deployable deep learning models for volumetric medical image analysis.


### Abstract

Three-dimensional (3D) medical imaging has become indispensable for disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and longitudinal monitoring, yet the computational demands of modern deep learning models remain a major obstacle to their clinical deployment. Knowledge distillation (KD) has emerged as an effective strategy for transferring knowledge from high-capacity teacher models to lightweight student models, enabling efficient inference while preserving predictive performance. This paper presents a systematic review and comprehensive taxonomy of knowledge distillation methods for 3D medical imaging published between 2015 and 2025. Following the PRISMA-ScR framework, we analyze the current literature and organize existing approaches according to the transferred knowledge, teacher–student configuration, supervision strategy, data availability, learning paradigm, execution mode, imaging modality, and downstream clinical task. Beyond model compression, we examine the expanding role of KD in applications such as semi-supervised learning, multimodal learning, missing-modality learning, domain adaptation, uncertainty estimation, and federated learning. We further summarize methodological trends, compare applications across major 3D medical imaging tasks, identify persistent challenges related to benchmarking, reproducibility, and clinical validation, and highlight promising directions for future research. By providing a unified taxonomy and structured analysis of the field, this review serves as a practical reference for researchers and practitioners developing efficient and clinically deployable AI systems for volumetric medical imaging.
